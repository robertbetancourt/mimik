# VISUAL_LANGUAGE

## Visual Goal

Premium.

Warm.

Playful.

Editorial.

Friendly.

Expressive.

Timeless.

---

## Design Philosophy

The interface stays calm.

The illustrations create the fun.

Every screen should feel clean enough to let Mimik become the emotional focus.

---

## Experience

Players should feel:

- Welcome
- Comfortable
- Curious
- Excited
- Ready to play

Never overwhelmed.

Never distracted.

---

## Visual Style

- Warm light background
- Minimal interface
- Editorial composition
- Large whitespace
- Rounded components
- High-quality illustrations
- Soft color palette

---

## Two Stages

Mimik has two visual modes. Every screen belongs to exactly one.

### Backstage — warm and calm

Home, game setup, player editing, turn results, history, settings.

This is where people configure and read. It stays warm, light, editorial
and quiet, so the illustrations carry the personality.

### Stage — dark and loud

Countdown, gameplay, podium.

This is the show. A near-black stage (`#0B0912`) lit by two drifting
lights in the category's accent colors (`src/features/gameplay/stage/palette.ts`).
High energy, saturated, cinematic. The phone is held up for the room to
see, so everything must read from across a living room.

Rules on the stage:

- The word is always white (gold for golden words), huge, uppercase and
  centered on the screen itself — never on the space the HUD leaves over.
- The timer is the screen frame, not a widget.
- Color shocks (green correct, orange pass, gold ×2, red urgency) are
  reserved for game events — never decoration.
- HUD sits in the corners and never shares the center with the word.
- Mimik watches from a corner and reacts; it never covers the word.
- Roadtrip Mode turns every effect into a simple fade.

---

## Color

Backstage colors should feel:

- Warm
- Soft
- Cheerful

Avoid highly saturated backstage interfaces — illustrations provide the
strongest colors there.

On the stage, saturation is the point: the category accents act as stage
lighting on a dark background, and the word stays white on top.

---

## Composition

Every screen should breathe.

Prioritize:

- Whitespace
- Clear hierarchy
- Large touch targets
- Comfortable spacing

Illustrations may break their containers to create depth and personality.

---

## Cards

Cards are small scenes.

Not simple containers.

Each category should tell a small visual story.

Some categories include Mimik.

Others use original illustrations built in the same visual language.

---

## Illustrations

Illustrations are the main source of personality.

Style:

- Rounded
- Friendly
- Expressive
- Dynamic
- Highly readable

Illustrations should feel collectible.

---

## Mimik

Mimik is never decoration.

Mimik appears only when it adds emotion.

Examples:

- Countdown
- Celebration
- Victory
- Funny failures
- Waiting states
- Turn summary

---

## Typography

Style:

- Modern
- Rounded
- Highly legible

Preferred families:

- Satoshi
- Sora
- Plus Jakarta Sans
- Urbanist

Typography should communicate clarity before personality.

---

## Components

- Minimal
- Rounded
- Lightweight
- Consistent

Avoid decorative UI.

---

## Motion

Every animation has a purpose.

Motion should:

- Celebrate
- Guide
- Reward
- Surprise

Never distract.

Never delay gameplay.

On the stage, motion is the main source of energy: letters cascade in,
correct words fly up and burst, passed words fall, the frame burns down,
the vignette beats in the last ten seconds and the music speeds up. Every
feedback animation still fits inside the 400 ms feedback window.

---

## Personality

The interface is elegant.

Mimik is expressive.

The contrast between both creates the product identity.

---

## Avoid

- Glassmorphism (backstage; subtle glass cards are fine on the dark stage)
- Heavy gradients on backstage screens
- Excessive shadows
- Visual noise
- Overdesigned layouts
- Decorative animations
- Generic game UI
- Overly childish aesthetics

---

## Inspiration

Editorial mobile apps.

Nintendo.

Duolingo.

Modern product design.

Premium illustration systems.

---

## Visual Rule

The interface should disappear.

The people should remember the game.

The game should remember Mimik.

---

## Character Usage

Mimik is not the interface.

Mimik is not every illustration.

Mimik appears intentionally.

The world should feel alive even when Mimik is absent.

Categories should tell stories through environments, objects and composition, not only through the mascot.
